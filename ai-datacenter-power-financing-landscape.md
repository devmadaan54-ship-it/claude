# Financial Firms in the U.S. AI Data Center Power Domain

**A landscape map of the capital providers financing electricity for AI infrastructure**

Compiled August 2026. All figures are as reported by the sources cited at the end of each section.

---

## Scope and how to read this

"AI data center power" is not one market — it is a capital stack. A single gigawatt-scale
campus can involve six or seven distinct types of financial firm at once: an infrastructure
sponsor taking development equity in the generation asset, a private credit fund writing the
construction loan, a bank syndicate arranging the term debt, an ABS desk terming it out into
the securitization market, an insurer buying the resulting paper, and an advisory boutique
running the process.

This list is organized by **role in that stack**, not alphabetically, because the same firm
name means very different things in different rows. Blackstone as owner of a 744 MW gas plant
is a different exposure than Blackstone as a data center lender.

**Inclusion criterion:** the firm has committed capital, arranged capital, or advises on
capital specifically tied to *powering* AI/hyperscale data centers in the United States —
generation, behind-the-meter assets, grid interconnection, transmission, storage, or the
utilities and IPPs serving those loads. Firms that only own the data center real estate
without a power angle are noted but not emphasized.

**Domicile note:** the user's question is about the U.S. *market*. Several of the most active
firms are foreign-domiciled (MUFG, SMBC, Natixis, Macquarie, EQT, Brookfield, Societe
Generale). These are marked 🌐 — they are central to U.S. deal flow and excluding them would
misrepresent the market.

## Market context (why the list looks the way it does)

- U.S. data center power demand: **31 GW (2025) → 41 GW (2026) → 66 GW (2027)** projected.
  Deloitte projects AI data center demand could reach **123 GW by 2030**.
- Private infrastructure funds raised a record **$250.7B** in 2025, up ~150% from $98.8B in
  2024; ~**$1.7T AUM** with roughly **$400B of dry powder** as of September 2025.
- Private equity investment in U.S. data centers hit **$45.7B in 2025** — 3× the prior year,
  and **72%** of the $63.35B total invested.
- Data center securitization outstanding grew from **$4B (2020) to $61B (YTD 2026)**;
  sell-side desks project **$30–40B/year** of gross supply in 2026–27, with Morgan Stanley
  estimating **~$130B** of U.S. net securitized-credit issuance across 2026–28.
- Morgan Stanley projects private credit will supply an additional **~$800B** of data center
  financing.
- **50+ commercial banks** are now active in the space; the North American data center
  financing market roughly doubled year over year.

## 1. Mega-cap infrastructure & private equity sponsors

These are the firms writing equity checks into generation assets, utilities, and integrated
"power + compute" platforms. This is the center of gravity of the domain.

### Blackstone
The most power-forward of the large sponsors, and unusual in owning generation outright.
- **$25B+** committed to a Pennsylvania digital/energy hub (data centers *and* gas plants).
- **Potomac Energy Center / Stonewall** — 744 MW gas plant, Virginia (acquired Aug 2025).
- **Hill Top Energy Center** — 620 MW combined-cycle gas, Western Pennsylvania (Sept 2025).
- **TXNM Energy** — $11.5B agreement to acquire the utility serving ~800,000 customers in
  Texas and New Mexico.
- **NIPSCO** — 19.9% stake (June 2024); proposed 2,300 MW gas plant via NIPSCO Generation.
- **PPL Corp** — power generation joint venture.
- Led the **$5.34B Williams behind-the-meter package** (see Williams entry below).
- Data center side: QTS Realty, AirTrunk, Digital Realty JV, $16B financing for the Related
  Digital 1 GW+ Oracle campus in Saline Township, Michigan.
- Vehicles: Blackstone Energy Transition Partners, Blackstone Infrastructure, BXCI (credit).

### Brookfield Asset Management 🌐 (Canada; major U.S. platform)
- **$10B AI Infrastructure Fund** launched 2025, targeting up to **$100B** deployed across
  data centers, **power generation**, and compute.
- **$100B AI Infrastructure Program** (Nov 2025) alongside NVIDIA and the Kuwait Investment
  Authority; ~$5B of equity commitments raised to date.
- Brookfield Renewable is a counterparty on hyperscaler PPAs; Brookfield Infrastructure holds
  Centersquare (~320 MW across 50 sites, from the Cyxtera/Evoque merger).
- Named in Sen. Warren's Oct 2025 probe of PE data center ownership.

### KKR
- **Helix Digital Infrastructure** — platform with **$10B+** in commitments for AI data
  centers, **power generation**, and connectivity.
- **KKR + Energy Capital Partners (ECP) partnership** — announced targeting **$50B** of
  investment in data centers *and* the generation to supply them. This is the archetypal
  "power + compute" pairing in the market.
- **$1B+ Bosque County, Texas** co-located power/data center project with CyrusOne and
  Calpine.
- **$20B+** deployed in power and renewables; $34B of equity across 23 digital infrastructure
  investments; five data center platforms, ~15 GW pipeline.
- Participant in the **$5.34B Williams** behind-the-meter financing.
- Named in Sen. Warren's probe (CyrusOne, acquired with GIP for $15B).

### Apollo Global Management
- **$40B+** deployed in 2025 across next-generation data centers and infrastructure.
- Majority stake in **Stream Data Centers**.
- **$3.5B** for the Valor/xAI facility.
- Participant in the **$5.34B Williams** behind-the-meter financing.
- One of the largest originators of data center debt (alongside Blue Owl, PIMCO, BlackRock),
  through both SPV and direct-lending structures.
- **$3B** infrastructure financing partnership with Standard Chartered for transition assets.
- Athene (Apollo's annuity arm) is a key balance-sheet buyer of the resulting paper.

### BlackRock — including Global Infrastructure Partners (GIP)
The largest single accumulation of generation capacity tied to the AI thesis.
- **AI Infrastructure Partnership (AIP)** — founded with GIP, MGX, Microsoft, later joined by
  **NVIDIA and xAI**. Targets **$30B of equity**, up to **$100B including debt**, explicitly
  for "data centers *and energy infrastructure to create new sources of power*." Raised
  $12.5B toward it. **GE Vernova and NextEra Energy** are named collaboration partners for
  scaling energy solutions.
- **AIP/MGX/GIP acquisition of Aligned Data Centers** — ~**$40B** enterprise value, ~5 GW,
  expected to close H1 2026.
- **AES Corporation** — GIP- and **EQT**-led consortium agreed (March 2, 2026) to acquire AES
  at $15.00/share: **$10.7B equity value, ~$33.4B enterprise value**. Stockholders approved
  June 26, 2026; close expected late 2026/early 2027. AES has **11.8 GW** of signed clean
  power agreements with major technology firms.
- **ALLETE / Minnesota Power** — acquired October 2025.
- Named in Sen. Warren's probe; PESP estimates the post-AES portfolio at ~50 fossil plants
  and ~32,000 MW across 11 countries.

### Stonepeak
- **Cleco** — agreed April 27, 2026 to acquire the Louisiana regulated utility (~298,000
  customers) from a Macquarie-led consortium alongside **Bernhard Capital Partners**, at a
  ~$5.75–6B valuation. Stonepeak takes the majority interest. A clean example of infra
  capital buying regulated rate base with data-center-driven growth.
- ~$40B capex program; targeting $10–15B of capital raises in 2026.
- Digital infrastructure positions including Princeton Digital Group ($1.3B preferred).

### EQT 🌐 (Sweden; large U.S. presence)
- Co-lead of the **AES** consortium (above) via EQT Infrastructure VI (€21.5B).
- **Scale Microgrids** — portfolio company, distributed generation directly relevant to
  behind-the-meter data center supply.
- **EdgeConneX** — portfolio company driving one of the largest single-site power financings
  in the market (see banks section).
- CEO has pledged **$250B+** of U.S. investment over five years.

### Macquarie Asset Management 🌐 (Australia)
- **$17B** in data center/digital infrastructure investment in 2025.
- **$5B partnership with Applied Digital** for HPC data centers; $900M Ellendale campus.
- Led the consortium (with **British Columbia Investment Management Corp** and **Manulife
  Investment Management**) that owned Cleco and is now selling to Stonepeak/Bernhard.
- Sold its Aligned stake into the $40B AIP transaction.

### DigitalBridge

- **$115B AUM**; $6.9B deployed in 2025; DigitalBridge Partners III with $11.7B of

## 2. Energy-specialist private equity and development capital

These are the firms whose core competence is power, now pointed at data center load. They tend
to sit closer to the actual megawatts than the mega-cap generalists.

- **Energy Capital Partners (ECP)** — the marquee name in power-for-data-centers, via the
  **$50B KKR partnership**. Owned by Bridgepoint.
- **ArcLight Capital Partners** — gas, pipeline, coal and generation assets; being acquired by
  DigitalBridge.
- **NGP Energy Capital** (Carlyle-affiliated) — co-founded **Cloverleaf Infrastructure**
  ($150M of a $300M raise), an energy-first data center site developer that has developed and
  sold **5+ GW** of projects.
- **Sandbrook Capital** — the other $150M in Cloverleaf; also backs **KRIOS Infrastructure**
  (grid-secured land for GW-scale AI campuses). One of the clearest "energy-first digital
  infrastructure" theses in the market.
- **Quinbrook Infrastructure Partners** — energy transition specialist with a dedicated data
  center/power convergence strategy.
- **Copia Power** (Carlyle) — utility-scale solar, storage and thermal paired with data
  centers.
- **EnCap Investments / EnCap Flatrock Midstream** — gas supply into power demand growth.
- **Oaktree** (Brookfield) — opportunistic and credit exposure to power assets.
- **Crestview, Riverstone, Tailwater, Pearl Energy** — mid-market energy sponsors with
  generation and gas assets increasingly marketed on the data center thesis.
- **Global Infrastructure Partners** — listed separately above under BlackRock.

## 4. Commercial and investment banks — project finance and syndicated lending

More than 50 commercial banks are active. These are the demonstrably active arrangers on
gigawatt-scale, power-inclusive deals.

### U.S. banks
- **JPMorgan Chase** — co-lead (with MUFG) on the **$22–23B Vantage Data Centers** loan, the
  largest data center financing on record. Estimates hyperscaler capex at **$697B in 2026**.
  Active on Oracle "Project Jupiter" debt.
- **Bank of America** — **$250B U.S. infrastructure financing pledge** covering Jan 1, 2026 –
  July 4, 2027, explicitly framed around power, not just GPUs. Active on Oracle data center
  debt.
- **Goldman Sachs** — underwriter on Vantage; projects **$765B** of AI capex in 2026 rising to
  **$1.6T annually by 2031**; publishes the leading research on private markets' growing role

- **Wells Fargo** — Vantage underwriter; broad power and project finance book.
- **Citi** — power/utility coverage and structured lending.
- **U.S. Bancorp (U.S. Bank Impact Finance)** — the dominant **tax equity and transferable tax

## 5. Securitization, ABS and structured credit

A fast-emerging and now distinct segment. Outstanding data center securitization grew from
**$4B (2020) to $61B (YTD 2026)**.

- **Underwriting desks:** Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, Bank of America, Barclays,
  Deutsche Bank, RBC Capital Markets, Guggenheim Securities, Truist, Wells Fargo.
- **Research shops setting the market's frame:** Barclays Research (the $61B outstanding
  figure), Morgan Stanley (the ~$130B 2026–28 net issuance estimate), RBC Capital Markets
  (securitization primers), the **Structured Finance Association (SFA)** and **CREFC** (the
  Data Center e-Primer, Feb 2026).
- **144A private placement market** — increasingly the gap-filler for what commercial banks
  cannot fully fund.
- **Regulatory development:** SEC guidance (July 29, 2026) drew a line between durable,
  actively managed operating assets and self-liquidating financial claims, giving qualifying
  data center transactions a path **outside Exchange Act ABS treatment** — a meaningful
  unlock for issuance volume.
- **Ratings agencies** shaping capital availability: Moody's, S&P Global Ratings, Fitch, KBRA,
  Morningstar DBRS.

## 10. Where the risk is concentrated

Worth stating plainly, because a list of names alone is misleading:

1. **Regulatory and political exposure is rising.** Senator Warren opened a probe (letters to
   **KKR, BlackRock, Brookfield Infrastructure Partners, and Blackstone**) into PE data center
   investments as retail utility costs rise. Rate-base allocation between hyperscalers and
   households is the live policy fight.
2. **Concentration.** A handful of firms — Blackstone, Blue Owl, Apollo, KKR, BlackRock/GIP —
   account for a disproportionate share of both equity and debt. Several are on multiple sides
   of the same deals.
3. **Fossil lock-in.** The behind-the-meter solution set is predominantly gas. The
   DigitalBridge/ArcLight and Blackstone gas plant acquisitions tie AI growth to long-lived
   fossil assets.
4. **Credit quality.** The same sponsors expanding fastest have publicly signalled wariness
   about underwriting standards in the sector.
5. **Counterparty concentration.** A large fraction of the debt ultimately depends on
   offtake from five hyperscalers plus OpenAI/xAI-type counterparties with shorter credit
   histories.
