# Deliverable 3 — Hidden Gems

Lesser-known institutions with genuine, evidenced sector activity that would not surface in a
conventional "top data center investors" search. All 40 below appear as rows in
`institutions.csv` with their source URLs; the evidence grade is repeated here.

## Specialist and emerging digital infrastructure managers

1. **Commonwealth Asset Management** (A) — Co-founded the USD 400m powered land platform launched with
   Silver Lake in August 2025; 3GW of power secured, sites in Texas and Georgia. Brand-new vehicle,
   no press profile of its own, operating at the scarcest point in the value chain.
2. **Chirisa Investments / Chirisa Technology Parks** (B) — Family-office-backed developer operating at
   multi-billion JV scale alongside Blue Owl Capital and PowerHouse Data Centers (JV of up to USD 5bn).
   Almost invisible in sector rankings.
3. **PowerHouse Data Centers** (B) — The data center arm of American Real Estate Partners; the third
   party in the Blue Owl / Chirisa JV and borrower on a USD 600m Northern Virginia development loan.
4. **StratCap** (B) — Surfaced only as Grain Management's co-buyer on a Toronto Tier III data center.
   Exactly the kind of name a transaction-first method catches and a list-first method misses.
5. **SDC Capital Partners** (B) — Founded 2017, ~USD 12.3bn of institutional capital, pure-play digital
   infrastructure, and almost no mainstream coverage.
6. **Palistar Capital** (B) — Digital infrastructure PE and structured investments with a named data
   centers and fiber sector focus; preparing a third fund.
7. **Grain Management** (A) — Washington DC minority-owned specialist. Preferred equity into Data Realty
   Holding (controlling shareholder of Prime Data Centers) and a majority stake in a Toronto data
   center; targeting ~USD 4bn for a new digital infrastructure fund.
8. **Post Road Group** (A) — Stamford CT. Post Road Digital Infrastructure Fund II (USD 500m, June 2025)
   is dedicated to a single data center platform — an unusual structure. Led the USD 187m DC BLOX
   financing.
9. **Tract Capital** (B) — Powered land developer behind the USD 4.6bn Fleet Data Centers secured notes
   offering; a capital markets counterparty most investor lists treat as a developer and skip.
10. **Northleaf Capital Partners** (B) — Seller side of the 2026 Vault Digital Infrastructure trade
    (7 US colocation assets, 75MW). Rarely named in US data center coverage.
11. **Igneo Infrastructure Partners** (B) — Buyer of that same stabilised US portfolio. Represents the
    yield end of the market, a different counterparty profile from development sponsors.

## Lower-middle-market and middle-market infrastructure PE

12. **NOVA Infrastructure** (A) — Took majority control of DartPoints in May 2025 as part of a USD 250m
    commitment; a Tier 2/Tier 3 market colocation thesis.
13. **Orion Infrastructure Capital (OIC)** (A) — Structured capital co-investor on the same DartPoints
    transaction; sits between equity and credit.
14. **Astra Capital Management** (A) — DC-based prior control sponsor of DartPoints, retained a minority.
15. **Argo Infrastructure Partners** (B) — >USD 6bn AUM and owner of TierPoint, a large multi-market US
    colocation platform. Almost never named in sector coverage.
16. **GI Partners** (B) — Twenty years of data infrastructure investing from San Francisco, including
    DRFortress and the 2025 purchase of Digita and IslandsTurnar from DigitalBridge.
17. **Madison International Realty** (A) — Invested in the USD 3bn Cologix recapitalization. A real
    estate secondaries and liquidity provider — a capital type almost entirely absent from sector lists.
18. **M/C Partners** (B) — Boston; communications and next-generation digital infrastructure; sponsor of
    Connectivity Wireless.
19. **Columbia Capital** (C) — Alexandria VA; reported assembly of a 2,069-acre Arizona data center
    campus parcel.
20. **Five Point Infrastructure** (A) — Houston energy PE that formed PowerBridge with a USD 1bn equity
    commitment and controls LandBridge's surface acreage. An energy firm that became a data center
    landowner.

30. **Engine No. 1** (A) — Activist-turned-industrial investor originating gigawatt-scale "power
    foundries" with Chevron and GE Vernova.
31. **Climate Adaptive Infrastructure** (A) — Larkspur CA; small climate infrastructure manager on the
    Intersect Power round that underpins Google's co-located clean power strategy.
32. **Greenbelt Capital Partners** (A) — Austin TX energy transition manager on the same round.
33. **Advanced Power** (A) — Now an ArcLight platform, with a USD 1bn initial equity commitment and a
    20GW enablement target.
34. **New Era Energy & Digital** (B) — Small-cap issuer consolidating ownership of Texas Critical Data
    Centers, a 1GW+ Permian campus.
35. **PowerBridge** (A) — Five Point platform with a USD 1bn commitment and a 2GW LandBridge campus

**Also worth flagging as small but real:** **Great Elm Capital Corp** (A) — a genuinely small BDC named

USD 40bn Aligned sale, and a separate coverage team from Citizens' own digital infrastructure group;
**Brown Gibbons Lang** (B) — stood up a new Digital Infrastructure investment banking team, one of the
clearest hiring signals found in this pass.

## Note on SEC Form D and ERA filings

Section 11 of the brief recommends leaning on SEC Form D and Exempt Reporting Adviser filings for this
section. That was attempted and **did not produce usable results in this pass**: EDGAR full-text search
is not reachable through the search tooling available here, and keyword searches returned 10-Q, 10-K and
497 filings from large listed managers rather than Form D filings from emerging managers. Every hidden
gem above is therefore evidenced from a transaction, a firm release or credible trade press instead.
Running the Form D / IAPD sweep directly against EDGAR remains the single highest-value way to extend
this list — see `self_audit.md`.
